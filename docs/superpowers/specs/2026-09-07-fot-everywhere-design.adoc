== The brief, traced

Each requirement has an id so later sections can cite it.

[cols="1,6",options="header"]
|===
| Id | Requirement (owner's words, 2026-09-07, condensed)
| R1 | "install hyperpolymath's FoT please" — any AI can say it, and FoT is wired in (via BoJ where BoJ exists) with a proper command line, a TUI and host integrations (Antigravity, VS Code, a game, whatever is going on).
| R2 | Nested extensions: the absolute minimum through the absolute maximum, chosen by the user or host.
| R3 | Not limited to boj-server; passable around anywhere — but with the safeguards already built in.
| R4 | Always useful, always helpful: never a way to fling abuse around or fill inboxes with the unworkable. Consider Reddit and customer forums, and the people on the receiving side.
| R5 | Sender and recipient may be a programming genius or a first-time user, a corporation or a natural person, an agent or a person, a child or an adult.
| R6 | Privacy: never send what the user does not want sent (build number, MAC, IP, or whatever they prefer), even when it would be useful; offer whatever is possible within the user's willingness; transparent at every point without over-warning; personalised per user, organisation or service.
| R7 | Do not stop people sending two. Stop abuse in volume.
| R8 | Priority honest in both directions: no "urgent" for the trivial, no "standard" for life or death.
| R9 | Decide what model, what effort, and what advisor the tool uses.
| R10 | Build on what was working and established; find what never made it into practice; bring it up to date with the latest standards and rsr-template.

[[stocktake]]
== What exists, what was established, what never reached practice

_Measured 2026-09-07 on origin/main (commit 515ee45, 259 commits, history
intact; last engine commit 73ba800 on 2026-07-17). Tests: 272 pass on
Elixir 1.19 / OTP 27._

=== Established and worth keeping

* The engine under `elixir-mcp/lib/feedback_a_tron/synthesis/`: template
  fetcher and cache, intent classifier, hydrator, research, synthesizer, form
  validator and renderer — all real, none stubbed, all tested. Plus a
  deduplicator with text similarity, a supervised rate limiter, a hostility
  filter (`IntentClassifier`: rejects zero-signal abuse, salvages the
  actionable core of mixed text), fourteen channel adapters (GitHub, GitLab,
  Bitbucket, Codeberg, Sourcehut, Bugzilla, Jira, Discourse, Reddit, Discord,
  Matrix, email, Mailman, NNTP) and a submitter with an audit log. Reddit and
  Discourse already exist, so the forum destinations in R4 are a wiring
  question, not a build. This is the core and it stays the core.
* A GenStage migration-observation pipeline (producer → review consumer →
  VeriSim writer) with a batch reviewer and report generator, built for the
  ReScript migration. Real and tested, narrow in purpose, and the natural
  seed for burst clustering at L5.
* The MCP server (`mcp/server.ex`, stdio by default, optional loopback TCP)
  exposing three tools: research, synthesize, submit.
* The HTTP intake (`http_intake/router.ex`, 127.0.0.1:7722, off by default)
  with health, submit, research and synthesize routes.
* The Idris2 contract `src/abi/FeedbackOTron/Contract.idr` (total, no holes)
  as the specification of the form validator.
* The foundry's capability-partition vocabulary in boj-server-cartridges
  (granted capabilities, "inertness"), the right words for what an FoT
  extension may touch.
* The autonomous-bug-pipeline design (C1–C4) with its provenance discipline;
  C1 and C2 are backed by code and tests, C3 half (the contract exists but
  nothing checks the Elixir against it), C4 honestly marked partial.

=== Never reached practice

* *No model.* "Synthesis" is keyword and regex classification, YAML template
  parsing and shelling out to `gh`. No language-model call exists anywhere,
  no provider abstraction, nothing pluggable. The intelligence the product
  story assumes was never built. (This is the largest gap.)
* *No install.* `setup.sh` installs `just` and stops. The Justfile has no
  `mix`, `elixir` or `escript` recipe; `mise.toml`, `guix.scm` and
  `stapeln.toml` were copied from a template and declare Node, Python,
  Rust, Go, Zig, Java, Bun, Deno and Julia — never Elixir or Erlang. The
  `Mustfile` calls a `just test` recipe that does not exist. The only
  runnable artefact is a 3 MB prebuilt escript committed to the tree.
* *CLI is a stub.* Only `submit` is implemented; the CLI cannot research or
  synthesize. Help text says `feedback-o-tron`, the build says
  `feedback-a-tron`.
* *No TUI.* The `owl` dependency is declared and never used.
* *No host door other than boj.* In this document a _door_ is any adapter
  onto the core, and a _host door_ is the wrapper a specific AI host or
  editor loads. Two protocol doors exist (the stdio MCP server and the HTTP
  intake above); the only host door is the `bug-filing-mcp` cartridge in
  boj-server-cartridges. This repo has the wire, not the wrapper: no editor
  extension, no Antigravity or Claude Code stanza, no native SDK.
* *No privacy, consent or priority code.* No redaction or PII module; no
  consent or transparency module; severity is a pass-through field. The
  owner's safeguards exist as the rate limiter, the deduplicator, the
  hostility filter and the contract — real, but a third of the set.
* *Proofs and FFI unwired.* No NIF or port; `ffi/zig/src/main.zig` refers to
  `src/abi/Foreign.idr` and `Types.idr`, which do not exist. The contract's
  guarantees are mirrored by hand in Elixir, unverified.
* *Docs ahead of code.* README lists a standalone CLI among four senders
  (it cannot synthesize); says tokens are never stored in files while
  `credentials/file_store.ex` reads netrc and TOML; cites 2026-02-07 "real
  Bugzilla submissions" with no artefact; `mix docs` points at Markdown files
  that no longer exist; `migration_observe` is an unregistered MCP tool.
* Forge-side clustering, the org-level template fallback, OCI/SWR/OTel
  (boj #275/#276/#277).

=== Why it went backwards

Nothing was deleted: no commit since 2026-07-09 removes or stubs code. It
went backwards in *usability*, for five reasons.

. *The runtime policy moved under the docs.* The documented start path was
  Deno/npm (`npx @hyperpolymath/boj-server`). Deno and TypeScript were then
  removed from the estate's product and runtime path (boj #314, cartridges
  #126), so every recorded recipe stopped working, and nothing replaced them
  as a shipped artefact. Remnants outside that path are listed in
  <<stocktake>>: `examples/web-project-deno.json`, `mise.toml`, and the
  foundry's `mint.ts`, which <<standards>> assigns to SP1b and SP7.
. *Nothing ever shipped.* FoT is source plus two daemons that nobody starts,
  with template manifests that do not know it is an Elixir project. A tool
  with no artefact is only "installed" on the machine where someone last
  ran `mix`, and today that is no machine: 7700 and 7722 are both down.
. *CI went red and stayed red.* Callers granting less than the reusables
  request, a pin off standards main and an unpinned tag killed every run at
  startup with no jobs, so the failure was invisible; the two earlier
  repair PRs each fixed part of it.
  Nothing merges, so nothing lands, and the engine has not been touched
  since 17 July.
. *Work happened in throwaway clones.* The June IDE session minted two
  cartridges in a non-canonical clone and never committed; the clone was
  removed in September. The canonical clone sat on a stale branch (four
  unpushed pin-bump commits from 13–14 August, 17 commits behind main) and
  then took blanking-sweep damage. Note: a shallow fetch made that branch
  look 246 commits adrift with no merge base, and made a first stocktake
  conclude the history had been erased; both figures above are measured
  after deepening the fetch.
. *The docs say BUILT.* "LARGELY BUILT" is true of modules and false of the
  product: there is no model, no install and no CLI beyond submit, and the
  proof that matters — a stranger installs it and files one useful report —
  has never been run.

[[principles]]
== Principles

P1:: *The minimum is real.* Tier 0 must work with no model, no account and no
network beyond the destination itself, and must queue offline.
P2:: *A permitted provider first; the host's model where it is still lent.*
When FoT runs inside an AI host (Antigravity, Claude Code, VS Code, Cursor,
Zed, an agent framework) it uses a model provider the effective profile
permits (<<ledger>>): a configured cloud provider or a local endpoint. MCP
Sampling, the mechanism by which a server borrows the host's model, was
deprecated in the MCP specification revision of 2026-07-28 (SEP-2577); the
spec's migration guidance is direct integration with a provider API, and the
feature is eligible for removal in the first revision on or after
2027-07-28. FoT therefore treats sampling as opportunistic, never as the
default: detected at initialize, used only when the client negotiates a
revision from 2025-06-18 to 2026-07-28 inclusive and declares the `sampling`
capability, and retired when the spec removes it. `fot doctor` reports which
path it found. Own keys or a local model are the rule; a borrowed host model
is the bonus. (R1, R3) _Ruled 2026-09-08: accepted; see <<rulings>>. The
2026-09-07 draft said the reverse._
P3:: *Ledger before wire.* Nothing leaves the machine until the privacy ledger
for that report has been shown once and acknowledged; after that, the user's
standing preferences make it quiet. (R6)
P4:: *The recipient must be able to act.* A report that fails the
actionability predicate is returned to the sender with the one thing missing,
never sent "anyway". (R4)
P5:: *Priority is evidence, declared priority is a claim.* Both are recorded;
disagreement is resolved with one plain question; the safety path is never
downgraded and never batched. (R8)
P6:: *Two is fine, two hundred is a queue.* Volume is governed per sender and
destination; bursts are clustered, not dropped. (R7)
P7:: *One identity, many doors.* The engine, the contract and the policy are
one thing; CLI, TUI, MCP, HTTP, C ABI and BoJ are doors onto it. (R1, R3)
P8:: *Explain once, in the reader's register.* Transparency is a single clear
sentence at the moment it matters, in language chosen for the persona, not a
wall of warnings. (R5, R6)

[[architecture]]
== Architecture

[source]
----
                 ┌──────────────── doors ────────────────┐
  fot CLI ─┐     │ MCP server (stdio + streamable HTTP)  │
  fot tui ─┤     │ HTTP intake (127.0.0.1:7722)          │
  BoJ cart ┤ ──▶ │ C ABI (Zig) for games / native apps   │ ──▶ CORE
  editors  ┤     │ Gemini extension / VS Code / Zed …    │
  agents  ─┘     └───────────────────────────────────────┘
                                                            CORE = engine + contract + policy
  ┌──────── extensions (ladder L0–L5) ────────┐            policy = ledger · calibrator ·
  │ note · form · shape · evidence ·          │                     governor · personas
  │ advocate · fleet                          │            advisor = pre-send reviewer
  └───────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                                            ▼
  destinations: GitHub / GitLab / Codeberg issues & discussions · email · Discourse ·
  Zendesk / Freshdesk / HubSpot forms · schema-described web forms ·
  portable report file (when no channel is known)
----

Components:

Core:: The existing Elixir engine, the Idris2 contract, and the policy
modules (new). Runs as *one* OS process (`fot serve`) that hosts the intake,
the MCP transport and the engine — the 7700/7722 two-daemon dance ends.
Doors:: Thin adapters. Each door is < 300 lines and owns no policy. Protocol
doors (MCP, HTTP, C ABI) speak a wire; host doors (a cartridge, an editor
extension, a host stanza written by `fot doctor`) load a protocol door into
one particular host.
Extensions:: The ladder. Each level is a plugin with a manifest that declares
its granted capabilities in the foundry's vocabulary (Net, Fs, Clipboard,
Screen, Logs, Model) so a host or an organisation can cap the ceiling.
Destinations:: Adapters that know a channel's schema and its floor (what it
*requires*, e.g. GitHub issue forms' required fields, a vendor's form fields).
Policy:: Privacy ledger, priority calibrator, volume governor, persona
profiles. Policy is data (a signed profile per user, organisation or
service) interpreted by one engine; there is no per-door policy code.
Advisor:: A separate review pass with the full draft, ledger and evidence,
answering five fixed questions before anything is sent (<<model-advisor>>).

[[ladder]]
== The ladder: nested extensions L0–L5

Each level is a strict superset of the one below; a host chooses the ceiling,
a user chooses per send, and the ledger shows which level a report used.

[cols="1,2,4,3,2",options="header"]
|===
| Level | Name | Adds | Needs | Typical user
| L0 | Note | Free text + a target; queued offline; portable report file if no channel is known. | Nothing. No model, no account. | A child, a first-time user, an air-gapped machine.
| L1 | Form | Fetches the destination's schema (issue forms, support form) and fills the required fields; validates against the contract. | Network to the destination only. | Anyone who has ever been bounced by "please use the template".
| L2 | Shape | Intent classification, synthesis into the destination's register, deduplication, two-way priority calibration. | A model the profile permits: a configured or local provider, or host sampling where a client still offers it (<<model-advisor>>). With no permitted provider L2 degrades to L1 and says so. | Most humans and agents.
| L3 | Evidence | Consented capture: logs up to this point, tool reports, config, screenshots, reproduction steps — each through the ledger and redaction with a diff before send. Media goes through the media redaction path in <<ledger>>; until that path exists, L3 sends no media. | Model + granted Logs/Fs/Screen capabilities. | Developers, testers, agents reporting on their own runs.
| L4 | Advocate | Research first: is it known, fixed, worked around, discussed on the forum or Reddit? Suggest the answer before filing; cross-link; draft the recipient-side view. | Model + Net (read). | Support teams, maintainers, careful reporters.
| L5 | Fleet | Organisation and agent scale: budgets, clustering of bursts into one report with N attestations, multi-destination routing, receipts and tracking, audit trail. | Org profile + storage. | Corporations, agent fleets, the autonomous pipeline.
|===

The child's example lives at L0/L1: "I love airborne submarine squadron but
can you have different colours or let me scan in my own drawing" becomes a
feature request in the game's channel with the drawing attached, no free-text
personal data, and a confirmation in the child's register. The developer's
example lives at L3: logs to this point, tool reports and config, with the
build number, MAC and IP redacted because that user's standing ledger says so.

[[personas]]
== Personas and personalisation (R5, R6)

A *profile* is a small signed document, one each for the user, the
organisation and the service, layered in that order (service floor, org
policy, user preference; the most restrictive privacy setting wins, the most
permissive is never inherited upward).

Child:: Pictures and drawing upload; no free-text personal data collected;
guardian contact as the sender of record where the destination needs one;
playful, short confirmations; L0–L1 only unless a guardian raises the ceiling.
Novice:: Plain-language prompts, one question at a time, the ledger shown
as "here is exactly what will be sent", a preview in the destination's look.
Expert:: Raw structured input and output, batch mode, ledger as a diff,
keyboard-only TUI.
Corporation:: Org profile sets the floor and the budget; audit trail;
receipts; approval step optional; SSO deferred (<<yagni>>).
Agent:: Structured API with idempotency keys, machine-readable ledger and
receipts, explicit `declared_priority` and `evidence` fields, hard budget.
Recipient side:: Every destination adapter can express a *throttle* and a
*preferred shape* (for example "one issue per cluster, reproduction required,
no screenshots of PII"); FoT honours it and tells the sender why.

Register (reading level, tone, language) is chosen from the profile and can
be overridden per send. The same fact is never explained twice in a session
unless the user asks.

[[ledger]]
== Privacy ledger (R6)

Every datum FoT considers attaching is a *ledger row*:
`{class, source, value-summary, default, decision, reason}`. Every place a
datum can go is a *destination row*, and a model provider is a destination
like any other: nothing reaches a model that the ledger has not passed.

Classes:: identity (name, email, handles), device (build number, serials,
hardware ids), network (IP, MAC, hostnames), environment (OS, versions,
paths, usernames inside paths), logs, configuration, media (screens,
drawings), free text.
Defaults:: per class: `send`, `redact`, `ask`, `never`. The owner's own
standing rule — never build number, MAC or IP — is a user profile line, not a
special case.
Redaction:: deterministic first (structured scrubbers for IP/MAC/serial/
token/email/path-username patterns), then model-assisted only for free text
and logs, always producing a diff the user can see. Model-assisted redaction
never *adds* data, and it runs only on text the deterministic pass has
already scrubbed, so no model ever sees a raw IP, MAC or serial.
Media redaction:: screenshots and drawings take their own path: recover the
text in the image (OCR), run the same deterministic scrubbers over it, then
blur or block every matched region and show before and after as the diff.
Text the recogniser cannot read is treated as unknown, so an image with
unrecovered text defaults to `ask` and is never `send`. Media never goes to
a model-assisted pass. Until this path exists, no level sends media
(<<ladder>>, L3), and SP3's acceptance test plants an IP address in a
screenshot and proves it leaves blurred.
Model locality:: a profile line `model_locality` with values `any`,
`local-only` and `none`, evaluated *before* a provider is chosen
(<<model-advisor>>). Host sampling counts as cloud, because the host
forwards the payload to a vendor. The most restrictive value in the
service/org/user stack wins, like every other privacy setting.
Transparency:: the ledger is shown in full once per destination and per
level; afterwards a one-line summary ("sending 3 items, 2 redacted, nothing
from the *never* list") unless something new appears. Exactly what was sent
is stored locally and exportable.
Lifecycle of the local record:: the local record holds the *redacted*
payload as sent, the ledger rows, and a hash of each raw evidence item; it
never holds the raw evidence itself, so a `never` class is never on disk
under FoT's name. The store is one file per report under the user's config
directory, mode `0600`, encrypted at rest with a key kept in the OS keystore
where one exists (Secret Service, Keychain, DPAPI) and otherwise derived from
a passphrase the user chooses at first use. Retention defaults to 90 days,
after which the payload is dropped and only the ledger rows and hashes
remain; `fot ledger purge` (`--all`, `--before DATE`, `--report ID`) deletes
on demand; an org profile may set its own retention for reports sent under
it, and the user's own copy follows the user's setting. Export produces the
same redacted payload, never more.
Floor conflicts:: if a destination *requires* a class the profile marks
`never` (a vendor form that demands a serial), FoT says so in one sentence
and offers the portable-report route instead. It never sends it quietly.

[[calibrator]]
== Priority calibrator (R8)

Severity ladder with evidence predicates:

[cols="1,3,5",options="header"]
|===
| S | Meaning | Evidence that establishes it
| S0 | Safety of life, health, or a child; active exploitation | Any credible statement of harm; never requires reproduction.
| S1 | Data loss, security vulnerability, privacy breach | Repro or artefact showing loss/exposure.
| S2 | Blocked: cannot complete a core task, no workaround | Repro + failed workaround attempt.
| S3 | Degraded: works with a workaround | Repro + workaround.
| S4 | Cosmetic / minor | Observation.
| S5 | Wish / idea / praise | None.
|===

Rules:

* Both `declared` and `derived` are recorded on the report.
* Mismatch of one step is accepted silently in the sender's favour.
  Mismatch of two or more steps gets *one* question in the sender's register
  ("this reads as S3 — was anyone hurt or blocked?"); the answer is final.
* S0 is a fast path: it bypasses batching and clustering, goes to the
  destination's safety or security channel where one is known, notifies a
  human where the profile names one, and is never downgraded by any model.
* The fast path keeps one hard control, a *circuit breaker* per (sender,
  destination): after N S0 or S1 reports in a day (default 3) further S0/S1
  claims from that sender are not sent to the channel but routed to the
  human reviewer the profile names, or, where it names none, held as drafts
  with the sender told in one sentence. The breaker is never global, so one
  sender cannot close a safety channel for everyone, and it never lowers a
  severity: it changes who reads the claim next, not what the claim says.
  _Ruled 2026-09-08: accepted with N = 3; see <<rulings>>. The 2026-09-07
  draft let S0 bypass budgets outright._
* Persistent over-declaration by one sender lowers that sender's *auto-accept
  band* gently (the question appears sooner); at S2–S5 it never blocks
  sending, and at S0–S1 the circuit breaker above is the only limit. (R7)

[[governor]]
== Volume governor and anti-abuse (R4, R7)

* Token bucket per (sender, destination) and a daily budget per profile;
  defaults let a human send a handful an hour without ever meeting a limit.
* A burst is *clustered*: one report, N attestations, the sender told what
  happened. Nothing is silently dropped.
* Deduplication (existing) extends across destinations so the same defect is
  not filed to a forge and a support desk twice unless the sender asks.
* Tone: FoT proposes a rewrite for hostile text and asks; it never changes
  meaning silently and never sends content that targets a named individual.
* Recipient throttle signals (<<personas>>) are honoured and explained.
* Agents get hard budgets and idempotency; a fleet must present an org
  profile to exceed the human defaults.

[[usefulness]]
== Usefulness gate (R4)

Actionability predicate, added to the Idris2 contract:

* has an ask (a change, a question, a defect) that a reader can act on;
* has the environment at the level the destination's floor requires;
* is not a known duplicate (existing dedup) and, at L4, has been checked
  against the destination's own docs, changelog and forum — with the answer
  offered *before* filing when one exists;
* carries the priority pair from <<calibrator>>;
* passes the ledger.

A report failing the predicate is returned with the single missing item
named in the sender's register. "Send anyway" exists only at L0 (a note),
because at L0 the recipient has agreed to receive notes.

[[model-advisor]]
== Model, effort and advisor (R9)

Provider selection runs *after* the profile. The effective profile's
`model_locality` (<<ledger>>) first removes every provider it forbids; the
order among the rest is *configured provider* → *local endpoint* → *host
sampling where a client still offers it* → *none* (L0–L1 are fully
deterministic). Host sampling is detected, never assumed: the MCP initialize
handshake carries the client's protocol revision and capabilities, and FoT
uses sampling only on revisions 2025-06-18 through 2026-07-28 that declare
it; the feature is deprecated from 2026-07-28 and FoT drops the path in the
release after the spec removes it (<<principles>>, P2). `fot doctor --host`
reports which path it found and, for sampling, the revision negotiated.

*No permitted provider.* A `local-only` profile with no local endpoint
configured, or a `none` profile, has no provider at all. That is a defined
state, not an error: L2 and above degrade to L1 (form fill) and the
confirmation says so; the advisor and the model-assisted redaction pass do
not run; the report's ledger records `no permitted provider`; every
deterministic gate (scrubbers, contract, dedup, governor, actionability)
still applies; and each free-text and log row defaults to `ask`, shown in
full, instead of `send`, because the pass that would have checked it could
not run. The 2026-09-07 ruling that the advisor is on by default at L2–L3
therefore reads "on by default wherever a permitted provider exists".

[cols="2,3,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Stage | Default model when FoT runs its own | Effort | Notes
| Classification, redaction assist, register detection | Haiku 4.5 (`claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`) | low | Cheap, fast, runs on every send; deterministic scrubbers run first.
| Synthesis, hydration, recipient-side draft | Sonnet 5 (`claude-sonnet-5`) | medium | Escalate to high when derived severity ≤ S1 or level ≥ L4.
| Research (L4) | Sonnet 5 | medium | Read-only Net capability; sources cited in the report.
| Advisor (pre-send review) | Opus 5 (`claude-opus-5`); Fable 5.1 where available | high | Sees the full draft, ledger, evidence and profile, all post-scrub. Mandatory for S0/S1, L4+, and org profiles; on by default at L2–L3 (ruled 2026-09-07) with opt-out per profile, never per report; absent at L0–L1 and wherever no permitted provider exists.
| Local / private tier | Ollama or any OpenAI-compatible local endpoint | as configured | For `local-only` profiles; L2 quality degrades, ledger and gates do not.
|===

The advisor answers five fixed questions and may only *return* the report,
never edit it: Is it useful to the recipient? Is it safe (no harassment, no
targeting)? Is the priority pair honest? Does the ledger match the payload?
Is the register right for the recipient? A "no" comes back with the reason
in one sentence. Where a host still lends its model over sampling, the
advisor runs as a second sampling call with a different system prompt; where
possible it uses a different model family from the synthesiser so the two do
not share blind spots.

[[install]]
== Install and distribution (R1)

Naming, ruled 2026-09-07 and closed 2026-09-08: the product is
*Feedback-o-Tron*, a self-describing, whimsical name in the spirit of a
machine from a children's story, one that encourages people to make things
better. Renaming to `fot` was rejected. `feedback-o-tron` is the canonical
command and cartridge name, spelled `o-tron` on every surface; `fot`
survives only as a typing alias, and `fot` in this document is shorthand for
that command. The `a-tron` spelling is retired from every user-facing
surface (escript name, help text, catalogue entry); the internal Elixir
namespace `FeedbackATron` is renamed in a mechanical follow-up after SP1b.
The name is no longer open.

"Install hyperpolymath's FoT please" resolves, in order:

. *Through BoJ where BoJ is present:* `boj install feedback-o-tron` (the
  `bug-filing-mcp` cartridge is renamed `feedback-o-tron`, old name kept as
  an alias) pulls the release artefact and writes the host stanza.
. *Directly:* one release artefact per platform — an OTP release wrapped as a
  single executable — published on GitHub Releases and ghcr (the Elixir
  image, not the bridge; boj #272), with `mise` and Guix (`guix.scm` exists)
  as package routes. No Deno, no npm, no `curl | sh`.
. *Host wiring:* `fot doctor --host <host>` writes the MCP stanza for that
  host and verifies the handshake. SP2 ships four hosts, each with a
  handshake test: `antigravity`, `vscode`, `claude-code`, `gemini-cli`.
  `cursor` and `zed` follow after SP2 once their stanzas are verified the
  same way; until then `fot doctor` names them as not yet supported rather
  than guessing. Antigravity gets `wsl -e` handling on Windows; the Gemini
  catalogue entry is the existing `gemini-extension.json` bumped and
  submitted.
. *Games and native apps:* a C header and static library from the Zig FFI
  (`fot_send(json)`), delivered by SP8 in <<decomposition>>, plus the HTTP
  intake for anything that can speak HTTP.
. *Humans:* `fot` CLI (`fot send`, `fot draft`, `fot ledger`, `fot doctor`)
  and `fot tui` (a wizard that walks the ladder one level at a time).

`fot serve` is the only daemon, and the CLI starts it on demand.

[[proposals]]
== Proposals raised in review (2026-09-08), ruled the same day

Two changes in this revision altered design the owner approved on 2026-09-07.
Review raised them; they were written into the sections above marked as
proposed, and the owner accepted both on 2026-09-08 (<<rulings>>). The
reasoning is kept here. Neither is implemented.

. *P2 flipped: configured provider first, host sampling opportunistic*
  (<<principles>>, <<model-advisor>>). Reason: MCP Sampling was deprecated in
  the specification revision of 2026-07-28 (SEP-2577), with direct provider
  integration as the stated migration path and removal possible from
  2027-07-28; a design that leads with it would ship on a sunset feature.
  The rejected alternative was to keep host-first for the hosts that still
  declare sampling and accept the sunset.
. *S0 circuit breaker* (<<calibrator>>). Reason: a fast path that bypasses
  every budget lets one sender flood a safety channel with repeated claims,
  against R4 and R7; the breaker keeps the path fast and never downgrades a
  claim, but caps the count per sender and destination and hands the rest
  to a human. The rejected alternative was the 2026-09-07 text, in which
  nothing ever limits S0.

Two further additions under R6 are refinements rather than reversals and are
recorded here so the growth in SP3 is visible: the media redaction path and
the local-record lifecycle (<<ledger>>).
